# Supervisor Style Profile (v0.1, revised per PHASE 3A.1) — Operational Rules

Provenance tags on every rule:
- **[S]** directly observed supervisor-style pattern (corpus v0.1)
- **[J]** general journal/genre convention observed in the corpus
- **[I]** deliberate improvement introduced for clarity/publication quality
  (not a copied habit)

## Voice and formality
- [S] Formal, high-information academic register; explanatory compound
  sentences; minimal filler.
- [S] Write for expert reviewers; in applied contexts, also for
  industrial/regulatory readers.
- [I] One main idea per paragraph; front-load the paragraph's point.

## Rhetorical sequence
- [S] Default macro-move: context → gap → method/finding → implication.
- [I] This sequence is an **adjustable default**: follow the target venue's
  required structure whenever it differs (structured abstracts, funder
  templates, combined Results-and-Discussion, thesis formats).

## Problem framing
- [S] Quantitative/economic problem framing (costs, shares, magnitudes,
  national statistics) is characteristic of Persian proposals and applied
  research contexts.
- [S] For basic plant-biotechnology and ecophysiology papers, frame
  significance around biological, methodological, or monitoring value
  rather than injecting economic framing by default.
- [I] Do not auto-inject economic framing into English manuscripts; use it
  there only when field, venue, and data warrant it.

## Objectives and hypotheses
- [S] State one general objective in paragraph form, then specific
  objectives separately.
- [S] In proposals, enumerate explicit hypotheses and research questions.
- [I] Keep hypotheses testable and distinct from project justifications;
  keep hypothesis statements compact and tied to the design that tests them.

## Materials and Methods
- [S] Reproducibility-level parameter reporting: instrument + model/version,
  conditions, quantities, gradients, n, units.
- [S] Give the reason for non-obvious methodological choices in-line.
- [J] Past tense, passive-dominant; occasional first-person plural for study
  actions.

## Results
- [S] Figure-led narration ("Fig. X shows …"); key table values read into prose.
- [S] Quantitative statements with supplied values, dispersion, significance
  level as given.
- [I] Observation only in Results; interpretive material goes to Discussion.
- [J] If the target venue combines Results and Discussion, mirror that
  section structure while clearly separating observation from
  interpretation within the prose.

## Discussion
- [S] Mirror the Results' subsection order; per-finding move: our finding →
  comparison with literature → agreement/disagreement → mechanistic or
  practical explanation.
- [S] Comparison formulas (generic forms): agreement, contrast, attribution.
- [I] End Discussion with an explicit Limitations passage and a clearly
  prospective forward look.

## Conclusion
- [S] Short synthesis and implication; no new data; no new citations.
- [S] Where warranted, an elevation move that reframes the studied object in
  terms of its demonstrated value.
- [J] One prospective sentence, explicitly prospective.

## Sentences and paragraphs
- [S] Medium-long explanatory sentences with subordinated clauses; deliberate
  parallel lists for method steps and enumerated spectral/chemical regions.
- [I] In English, keep most sentences under ~35 words; split long
  Persian-derived sentences when translating.

## Verbs and transitions (categories only — never reuse corpus wording)
- [S] Reporting verbs: show / reveal / indicate / demonstrate / establish /
  confirm.
- [S] Comparative-discussion verbs: agree with / corroborate / contrast with /
  attribute to.
- [J] Standard connectors: however / in contrast / consequently / accordingly /
  moreover / specifically.

## Claim-strength calibration (replaces "always hedge")
- [I] Strong evidence (replicated, controlled, quantified) → direct
  statements.
- [I] Partial evidence → calibrated hedges.
- [I] Causal/general claims without causal design → cautious wording; never
  state causation from correlation.
- [S] The corpus's hedging vocabulary (may / can / appear / seem / suggest /
  likely) is the model for hedged cases.

## Citation placement
- [S] Citation-dense Introduction; parenthetical citations at claim ends.
- [J] Every prior-work claim cited; own-data claims uncited.
- [J] Follow target-venue rules for the abstract: no citations in the
  abstract unless the venue explicitly permits or requires them.

## Quantitative reporting
- [S] Values with units and dispersion exactly as supplied (mean ± SD, n);
  percentages of identified totals; field-standard performance metrics when
  they belong to the task (e.g., error metrics, kinetic parameters).

## Persian proposal vs. English manuscript
- [S] Persian proposals: funder-template architecture; bullet-heavy; executive
  first-person-plural voice; national/economic context (import substitution,
  cost of foreign standards, data sovereignty) is legitimate and expected
  **there only**.
- [S] English manuscripts: strict IMRaD unless the venue combines sections;
  shorter sentences; venue-format compliance; AI-use disclosure drafted only
  from the user's confirmed actual AI use and the journal's verified policy.
- [S] Persian→English = reconstruction: transfer ideas and structure, not
  sentence architecture.

## Common weaknesses to avoid
- [I] Blending interpretation into results (the corpus does this; the skill
  separates).
- [I] Hypotheses stated as if already supported.
- [I] Limitations omitted or buried mid-discussion.
- [I] Prospective applications phrased as achieved results.
- [I] Uncited prior-work claims; invented citations; unsupported superlatives.
- [I] Citations inserted into an abstract where the venue does not permit them.
